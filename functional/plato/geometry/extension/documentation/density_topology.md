The geometry component, `density_topology`, specifies a design field defined over the nodes of the provided input `mesh_name`.
These nodal values are referred to as densities (or pseudo-densities as they are not related to the mass density) and define an implicit material distribution for finite element analysis, with values ranging from 0, representing void, to 1, representing solid material.
Objective and constraint calculations account for this implicit representation of the geometry through interpolation and penalization of material properties.
As an implicit geometric definition, density topology can be used for many types of optimization problems but tends to struggle with physics that require a precise/explicit geometry definition for correctness and accuracy.
An example of this would be structural dynamics where intermediate density values (non 0 or 1) or islands of material can lead to incorrect results.

An explicit representation of the final optimized geometry can be generated by extracting the 0.5 density iso-volume from the density field using the optional parameter `cut_mesh_name`.
